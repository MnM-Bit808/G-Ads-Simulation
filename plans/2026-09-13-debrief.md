# Stage D1: the debrief

*Design, 2026-09-13. Decided with the owner one section at a time. No code has
been written against it. Recommendation 6 of `reviews/slc-review.md`, built on
the branch `stage-c`.*

*The quoted examples below show the shape of each sentence; their figures are
illustrative, not measured.*

## 1. Why

The end screen is an answer sheet plus a 60-line change log (review section 7).
Everything a debrief needs is already computed, and none of it is read back:

- `decisionLog` records every decision, and "What you did" lists them without
  saying what any of them did;
- `missedToBudget` is computed for every week a budget runs out, and no screen
  shows it, although DECISIONS G1 says "the UI must label it";
- the standings table reveals where each rival sat, as one column among eleven,
  so the positioning lesson (read the market from eight ads) lands, if it lands at
  all, in passing.

The review's gate for stage D: strangers can state three lessons in their own
words, and weeks 7-12 carry decisions. This document covers the first half; the
second (moving the capital window to week 7) is its own change.

## 2. The end screen's new order

1. *Twelve weeks*, the result, as today.
2. **New: What your twelve weeks taught you**: the moments (section 4), the
   budget "what if" and the lessons (section 5).
3. **New: Where did they sit?**: guess, then reveal (section 6).
4. *Everyone, with the books open*. **Its position column stays hidden until the
   reveal is answered or skipped**, or the answer sheet sits under the quiz.
5. *Every week*, *The account*, *What you did*, *Play again*, as today.

## 3. The pieces

| file | job | checked by |
|---|---|---|
| `ui/debrief/read.js` | pure readings: pick the moments and their before/after, read the budget weeks, decide the lessons, score the guesses | unit tests on played games |
| `game.js` → `replayWithout` | replay a game from its start with one advertiser's decisions for one round removed, everyone else's logged decisions unchanged | unit tests: nothing removed reproduces the game exactly; a removal changes nothing before its round |
| `ui/debrief/render.js` | draws the two new cards and runs the replay | playing it |
| `ui/words/debrief.js` | every sentence and label, under the content model's rules | `tests/words.test.js` |
| `ui/guide/chapters.js` | one new chapter, *What only a simulation can tell you* | `tests/guide.test.js` |

`renderEnding`'s context gains `initial`, the state before week 1, which
`ui/app.js` already keeps for the save. `serpBlock` is exported from
`ui/round-zero.js` so the reveal draws ads with round 0's own code.

**`replayWithout` belongs in `game.js`** because it is the only replay that
reproduces a played game (CLAUDE.md, known traps: `engine/serialise.js`'s
`replayGame` does not). The engine is untouched.

**The page stays responsive** the way the save restore already keeps it: the
replay pauses between weeks and reports "replaying week 5 of 12…". No worker, no
new machinery. A week replays in about 0.2 seconds in the browser.

## 4. The moments

**Candidates.** Every round with a player entry in `decisionLog`. Round 0 is not
one: there is no week before it to compare with. **Five or fewer are all shown;**
beyond five, the five with the largest relative change between the week before
and the week after, in clicks or in cost per click, ties to the earlier week.

**Each moment shows:**
- the change, in the words `summariseDecisions` already produces for "What you
  did" (one source for both cards);
- **the week before against the week after, in traffic only**: clicks, cost per
  click, spend, impression share (`traffic` and `share` on each report). Never
  conversions or profit: traffic is final when reported, while conversions and
  returns arrive for weeks, so a before/after on profit would measure how settled
  each week was and teach something false;
- what else changed that week, stated: always that rivals' bids moved too; and,
  when true, that a new month's budget began (weeks 5 and 9, from
  `ROUNDS_PER_MONTH`) or that capital landed (`budgetSchedule.capexAllocationRounds`).

**"What if I hadn't?"**, on demand, one moment at a time. It calls
`replayWithout` for that round and compares the two games.

**The window ends at the next change.** Keyword and negative lists are submitted
whole whenever either changes, so a later submission carries an earlier change
back in: a week-3 bid cut rides in again with a week-5 keyword. Removing week 3
therefore means something only until the player's next decision. The comparison
covers exactly those weeks (to week 12 if it was the last change), each read as
it stands at the end of the game in both versions, so both have had the same
time to settle:

> **Weeks 3-4, until your next change.** Without it you would have had 1,204
> fewer clicks, paid ₹4,100 more, and kept ₹12,300 less.
> Rivals make the moves they made in your game; in a real one they might have
> answered differently. **why?**

The last line is a disclosure, so it carries its "why?" to the new chapter
(section 5), and the chapter cites G1.

## 5. The budget "what if", and the lessons

### The budget "what if"

`missedToBudget` answers, for one week, what would have happened had that week
been allowed to spend the rest of the month: extra clicks, conversions, and
`profitIfFundedPaise`, whether the extra traffic would have made or lost money.

**The weeks are never added up.** Each assumes that week alone spent the whole
remaining month; two weeks in one month spend the same money, so a total would
count it twice. The card says instead:

> **Your budget ran out in 5 of 12 weeks.** In 3 of them more money would have
> paid for itself, week 6 most: 812 more clicks and ₹9,300 kept. In the other 2
> the extra traffic would have lost money, so running out saved you.
> *An exact figure only a simulation can have: Google Ads can only estimate it,
> as impression share lost to budget.* **why?**

A game whose budget never ran out is told that plainly: more money would have
bought nothing.

**The new chapter, *What only a simulation can tell you*,** is shared with the
moments' "What if I hadn't?". Its real half, on what Google reports instead
(impression share lost to budget, estimated extra clicks), is written from Google's
help pages fetched while drafting. Its game half cites G1 and G1a and says that
missed orders are costed at the expected return rate, because orders that never
happened cannot be returned. It is a concept chapter, not a device: the real
equivalent exists and is an estimate.

### The lessons

Six lines, each "you ran into this", with the player's own figure, or "you
didn't try this", with what to try next game. The wording is neutral: winning is
not the measure, and a sensible player finishes a median 6th of 9 (F15).

| lesson | met when | the line uses | why? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Numbers keep moving (I27) | always | week 1's gross profit as first reported against as it stands | `attributionLag` |
| Rank or budget (H3) | any week lost at least 10% of impressions to budget or to rank | the worst such week and which kind | when written |
| Broad match wanders (H5, H6) | any broad keyword was active | search terms bought that no keyword spelled out, and negatives added | when written |
| Returns: gross against kept (D4, I28) | always | returns' share of gross profit; rank on gross against rank on contribution | `returnsSplit` |
| Defending your name (A9) | a keyword on the player's own brand search | its cost per click against the rest of the account | when written |
| Smart bidding learns (D2) | the strategy was switched | the week, and the learning weeks that followed | when written |

**The 10% is a display number** and lives in the view with its reason, as
DECISIONS I6 records for numbers of that kind. A lesson whose chapter does not
exist yet gets its "why?" when C4 writes it.

## 6. The reveal

**The card, "Where did they sit?"**
- the eight rival ads exactly as read at round 0, from the starting state, drawn
  with round 0's `serpBlock`;
- under each, the six positions as a small grid, the same grid the player chose
  from; at least 44px a cell on a phone;
- **Reveal**, usable at any time (anything unguessed counts as skipped), and
  **Skip, just show me**, which reveals without a score.

**The reveal**
- each rival: the guess against the truth, exactly right, half right (price or
  purpose, and which), or wrong;
- a score: "You read 5 of 8 exactly, and 2 more half right";
- once: who sat in the player's own cell, "Marlow and Nira sat in your cell, so
  they bid on exactly the searches you did", or that nobody did;
- then the standings' position column appears.

**The truth comes from the starting state**, the positions the ads were written
for. Rivals never reposition today; reading the start means one that ever does
cannot make the reveal disagree with its own ad. A test checks the standings
column agrees with it.

**Motion, once per game, the review's rare-tier delight:** results appear row
by row, 50ms apart, each fading up about 4px over 200ms on
`cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)`, never blocking reading or scrolling; under
reduced motion, a fade without stagger or movement. The diff goes to the owner
for `/review-animations` before it is committed.

**Guesses are not saved.** A reload at the end screen starts the card afresh; the
playtest recorder captures the taps where they would be studied.

## 7. Tests, and what they cannot see

`tests/debrief.test.js`, on games played in the test:
- moments: all shown when five or fewer, the top five by change when more, ties
  to the earlier week; traffic figures only; the new-month and capital flags on
  the right weeks;
- `replayWithout`: nothing removed reproduces the played game to the rupee;
  removing round N leaves every week before N identical; the window ends at the
  player's next decision round;
- budget: weeks counted, paid-for against lost-money split, never summed; the
  never-ran-out case;
- lessons: each rule on a game built to meet it and one built to miss it;
- reveal: exact, half on price, half on purpose, wrong, skipped; the shared-cell
  line with and without a sharer; truth from the starting state; the player's
  own ad never among the eight.

Words and the new chapter go through the existing words and guide tests.

**By playing** on the dev server: a game with several decision weeks to the end;
every "What if I hadn't?" run and its window read; the budget card and the
lessons checked against the dashboards seen in play; guessing, revealing and
skipping, the column appearing; keyboard; phone width; the console.

## 8. Build order

Each step a commit after a green `npm test`, on `stage-c`.

1. `replayWithout` in `game.js`, tests first.
2. `ui/debrief/read.js`: moments, budget, lessons, reveal scoring, tests first.
3. The chapter *What only a simulation can tell you*, sources fetched while
   drafting; owner review.
4. `ui/words/debrief.js` and `ui/debrief/render.js`: the debrief card, then the
   reveal card and the hidden column; played.
5. The reveal's motion; the owner runs `/review-animations`.
6. Docs: HANDOVER, the published file count.

## 9. Risks

| risk | guard |
|---|---|
| a before/after read as cause | traffic only, "what changed", the confounders stated, and the replay for cause |
| a counterfactual that claims more than it measured | the window ends at the next change and says so; rivals held to their logged moves, and the screen says so |
| budget weeks summed into money that was never there | never summed; a test holds it |
| lessons that grade the player | neutral wording; met means encountered, not mastered |
| the reveal's answer visible before the quiz | the standings column hidden until reveal or skip |
| the end screen freezing during a replay | on demand, one moment at a time, pausing between weeks |
