Everyone’s books — three ways

Real figures from a game played through to week 12 on today’s build (Marlow, mid·performance, finished 8th). Every option carries the same eleven numbers per brand. What differs is how many of them you are asked to read at once.

What is wrong with it today
  • Six figures arrive as one sentence. “spent ₹4,81,159 · 1,482.6 conversions, ₹325 each · ₹4.62 of sales after returns per rupee of clicks · gross profit ₹13,97,433 · net profit ₹6,26,417 before the ₹0 it built with” — 12px grey, full width, nine times over.
  • The bars vanish on most laptops. They need a container over 900px, which needs a viewport over about 1295px. At 1280 the one visual in the section is display: none and the table is seven columns of numbers.
  • “Where they sat” is a dead column of dashes until the quiz is answered — 249px of nothing.
  • Fractional orders. “1,482.6 conversions”, “1,245.0 conversions”. Expected mode makes a sale a fraction; a shopkeeper counts whole ones.
  • “before the ₹0 it built with” on every brand that built nothing, which is most of them.
Option A

A card for each brand

The table becomes nine cards. Every figure gets its own label, so nothing is a run-on sentence and nothing needs width to survive — three across, two on a tablet, one on a phone. Ranking is still the order, but it reads as a gallery rather than a league table, and nine cards is a lot of scrolling.

Option B

The league table, with the detail one click down

Five columns you can scan: place, brand, the bar, what the business made, what returns took. The other six figures live under the row, opened by clicking it — and your own row is open from the start. The ranking stays a ranking; the depth is there when wanted. The bar survives at every width because it is the third column rather than a luxury.

Option C

The chart first, the books under it

Two things doing one job each. A ranked chart where the gap between outline and fill is what returns took — the lesson of the whole section, drawn once at full width. Then a plain table of the figures with real column headings. Nothing is hidden and nothing is a sentence, but it is two passes over the same nine brands.
