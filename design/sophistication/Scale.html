The Studio’s scale — three depths

The same two cards, drawn four ways. Real figures from the saved game at week 4. Nothing here is a new visual language — it is the one already in the Studio, with the accidents taken out of it.

What the measurement found

25 distinct font sizes. The ones in red appear once or twice each — they are drift, not decisions:

10×1010.5×111×5212×9112.5×1 13×6113.5×214×4115×1316×4 17×318×119×120×2 22×124×330×234×1

Display sizes add nine more: 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 22, 24, 26, 28, 30, 40, 48 — with the same size carrying three different line-heights (20px/1.1, /1.15, /1.2). Eight non-pill corner radii: 2, 3, 4, 9, 12, 14, 20. Eight card paddings.

Today

As it is now

Card padding 16×20, radius 20, figures 26px, headings 18px. Nothing wrong with any one value — the problem is the neighbours it does not match elsewhere.

Option A

Tighten the scale, change nothing else

The 25 sizes collapse onto eight steps — 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 20, 24 — and the one-offs move to their nearest neighbour (10.5→11, 12.5→13, 13.5→13, 17→16, 19→20). One line-height per step. Eight radii become four: 4, 12, 20, 999. Spacing, layout and colour are untouched. Every individual move is one or two pixels; the whole is what stops reading as homemade.

Option B

Scale, plus one rhythm inside every card

A, and then every card gets the same internal rhythm: heading, then figures, then the reading, on a 4px spacing scale, with a slightly tighter corner. Cards stop being nine arrangements of the same parts and become one arrangement. Layout and type sizes are otherwise A’s.

Option C

Scale, rhythm, and a real focal point

B, and then the hierarchy is pushed: the figure that matters grows to 32px, the heading to 20px, and the supporting reading drops to a quieter grey. Each screen gets one thing the eye lands on first. This is the only option that changes how loud the page is — and the one most likely to need a second pass on screens with six figures competing.
