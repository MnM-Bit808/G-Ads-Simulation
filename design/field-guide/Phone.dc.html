Refused at the door, or sent back

In real Google Ads

An order can come back in two ways. A parcel that is not delivered, for instance because it was refused at the door, goes back to the seller; Indian e-commerce calls this RTO, return to origin, and there was never a sale. A delivered order can also be sent back later, and clothing is the most-returned category, at roughly 25–40%. Google Ads counts the purchase when it happens and learns of neither unless you upload an adjustment that retracts or restates it. Until then, the return on ad spend it shows you is before returns.

Sources:Pragma, on RTO chargesGoogle Ads Help, conversion adjustments
In this game

Here the returns card is your shop’s own books, which Google Ads would show only if you uploaded them. Every order can be refused at the door, 20.9% of the time when paid in cash and 5.8% when prepaid, and 30% of delivered orders are sent back. Each refused parcel also costs ₹200 in freight. The two halves have different fixes: a size guide reaches only send-backs, while order confirmation and a prepaid discount reach only refusals. The refusal rates come from one named study; newer reports put cash refusals higher.
